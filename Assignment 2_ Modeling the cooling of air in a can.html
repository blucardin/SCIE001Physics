Assignment Description

Similar to your first long-assignment, this long-form assignment has two main parts:

  1. Collect data: Use your Arduino and the DHT11 temperature sensor to record the temperature of a small volume of air in a beverage can as it cools down 
    • You may use any small aluminum cylindrical beverage can of your choosing (e.g. soda can, etc.)
    • Use any convenient heat source to heat the can and thus the air within it. You don't need much heating; 10 C above ambient is sufficient.
    • You should repeat your experiment three times with the same can; and thus obtain three temperature vs time datasets.  
    • Use the Jupyter notebook introduced in your pre-reading (L17_DHT_DAQ_jma_v2.ipynb Download L17_DHT_DAQ_jma_v2.ipynb) to acquire and save your temperature data to a .csv file. You can also use code from Assignment 1 to pick out the data you want to analyze from a larger Numpy array using indexing.  Make sure to keep some data before and after the cooling portion that you will fit so that the reader can get a better idea of what you were working with.

  2. Analyze data:  Use the Euler method to estimate the natural (unforced) convection coefficient kc and emissivity ε for your system by fitting a portion of your cooling data.
    • Use the file can_cooling_bare_bones.ipynb Download can_cooling_bare_bones.ipynb (in Module 3) as a basis for your analysis
    • Plot your best Euler model fit (as a solid line) along with your data (as points) on the same graph. All plots should include axes labels and a legend.
    • Find the temperature difference ΔT across the wall of your can
    • Repeat this process three times for each datasets.
    • Based on your three fits, report your best estimate (including uncertainties) for both the convection coefficient kc and the emissivity ε of your system with uncertainties.  

Submission details

  Your assignment submission should be single PDF document that includes three sections:

  • Introduction (max 100 words):
    • Explain what you did and state key facts about your experiment (e.g. describe your can, heat source used, etc.).
    • All reported quantities should have units and uncertainty (if measured).
    • Include a picture of your experimental setup.
  • Results (max 200 words)
    • A summary of how you analyzed your data (e.g. what differential equation is solved with the Euler method? how did you determine what portion of your data to fit? how did you determine your best fit parameters? what assumptions did you make? ...)
    • Equations, units and variables should be properly formatted (e.g. use latex or an equation editor).
      • Equations and variable definitions do not count towards word count.
    • Report the value of any constants used in your code in a table.
    • Report your fit parameters and the temperature difference ΔT across the wall of your can for each of your three trials in a table.
    • Report your final best estimate (including uncertainties) for both the convection coefficient kc and the emissivity ε of your system with uncertainties. Explain how you calculated these values. 
    • You must show your three plots as described above. Make sure the data in the graph as well as the labels and axes are easily readable in the PDF.
  • Discussion (max 200 words)
    • Provide some insight into what your plots are showing (this is where you demonstrate that you understand how the data relates to the physics we've learned in class!)
    • Discuss how well your fitting parameters across your three trials agree with one another.
    • Discuss if your final best estimates for convection coefficient kc and the emissivity ε are reasonable given your can and experimental conditions.
    • Discuss the significance of the temperature difference ΔT across the wall of your can

A note on captions: You need to label all figures and tables (e.g. "Figure 1" or "Table 1") and provide a short caption:

  • A caption should be max 2-3 complete sentences that provide enough detail to understand what the figure or table is showing (e.g. for a figure for PHYS 119 Lab 01, it's not sufficient to just say "A picture of my experiment" but "A spring of length 20cm (shown on the left) was compressed on a scale with 1 g precision (shown on the right) using my hand." would be more useful).
  • Titles on graphs are optional as long as your graphs are appropriately described by the figure captions.
  • You should always refer to the figures/tables in your text (e.g. "As seen in Figure 1, the spring constant was estimated by compressing a spring ...").
  • Captions do not count towards the word limit.

Collaboration and Academic Integrity

For all long-form assignments, you are encouraged to collaborate with others while collecting and analyzing your data. Working together to brainstorm ideas, troubleshoot experimental setups and debug code are all expected behaviors of professional physicists. While you are free to use any Python code that was provided to the class so far this term, the data you collect, the data analysis you perform on these data and your final written submission must be your own work. If you have more questions about this, reach out to someone on the teaching team and/or remind yourself of the course's academic integrity guidelines. 

Helpful tips

  • The same tips/warning from the first assignment apply here.  Make a plan and start early so that you have plenty of time to troubleshoot issues and ask for help.

  • For this assignment especially, do not underestimate the amount of time it will take to carry out a good analysis. Take some time to consider the differential equation that governs the rate of change of temperature for your system. You can show that all heat loss terms are proportional to temperature. So what shape should the cooling curve have? What does this imply for your fitting analysis?
